# Writing for the AI Domme

She is a person, not a script. Decision code never contains sentences: the engine decides *what*
she does (an utterance: an act, tags, slots, maybe an instruction) and the narrator picks the
words from line modules. You write those modules. Read `docs/PHASE-4.md` sections on the narrator
and the voice scheduler if anything below is unclear.

## A module

```json
{ "id": "chat.teasing", "act": "Chatter", "when": ["mood=Teasing"], "weight": 1,
  "cooldown": "Session", "variants": ["Close, aren't you? Shame."] }
```

- `id`: unique, dotted, grouped by act (`edge.watching.cruel`, `story.bedtime`).
- `act`: which moment it answers. The engine emits the act; you never add one without code.
- `when`: every condition must hold. Language: `key` (truthy), `!key`, `key=a|b`, `key!=a`,
  `key>=3`, `key<=3`, `key>2`, `key<2`, `#tag` (the utterance carries the tag), `#a|b`, `!#tag`.
  Keys are facts (mood, harsh, humiliation, manipulation, talk, edges, begs, segment,
  segment.steps, segment.tags, purpose, aim, role, closeness, need, control, overwhelm (her
  estimate of his state, 0 to 100), step.category, performer, tag, screen.now, orgasm.days,
  owed.edges, slipped, retaliating, ending.lean, ending.presentation, ...). Tags come from the
  engine: search for `Utterance.Of(SpeechAct.<Act>` in `Sessions/` to see which tags an act has.
- `weight`: relative to the other modules whose conditions hold. Give narrow, specific modules
  a higher weight so they win when they apply (2 to 5; up to 50 only when the line must win).
- `variants`: one is chosen. Or `lines` for a story (spoken one per quiet moment, in order).
- `cooldown`: `Session` (default: a variant is not repeated within a session), `Occasional`
  (about once in 5 sessions or 14 days), `Rare` (20 sessions or 60 days), `Once`.
- `spacing` (stories): seconds between lines. `noBridge`: the next order needs no bridge word.

## The three tiers

1. **Core**: the instruction itself ("Stroke up and down your cock with 1 hand, slowly, for a
   minute."). Generated by `Grammar`. Put `[Instruction]` where it belongs in an order line;
   a line without it is spoken before the instruction. Plain repeats are fine: "stroke" is
   "stroke". Never restate the method, pace or time in the wrapper.
2. **Stock**: short lines with several variants. They must not feel repeated within a session,
   so a module needs enough variants for how often its act fires (edge reactions: 4 or more).
3. **Stories**: multi-line, distinctive, memorable. Always `Occasional` or `Rare`. A repeated
   story is the fastest way to make her feel like a program, so write them sparingly and well.

## Her voice

- Short sentences. Terse is in character. One idea per line.
- No em or en dashes (validation fails), no double spaces, no lists, no "It's not X, it's Y",
  no therapist or assistant phrasing ("I understand", "Great job!").
- Persona sets the tone, not session severity: harshness, humiliation, manipulation and
  talkativeness are her dials (0 to 10). Gate strong humiliation with `humiliation>=6`, warmth
  with `harsh<=3`, mind games with `manipulation>=5`.
- Mood is dynamic (Neutral, Teasing, Playful, Edgy, Cruel, Relentless) and has causes
  (`mood.cause`: Begging, Pestering, Deal, Obedience...). Lines that react to the cause read as
  a person; lines that only name the mood read as a program.
- Slots in brackets: `[name]`, `[edges]`, `[left]`, `[performer]`, `[tag]`, `[owed]`, `[stakes]`...
  A capitalised slot (`[Edges]`) capitalises its value. Only use slots from `KnownSlots`, and
  only on acts that fill them (check the engine or Knowledge).
- Torture is intensity, not pain: torturous methods are overwhelming, almost unbearable, and he
  is desperate to stop. His whole body heats up and he can't focus. Never say it hurts, and don't
  call it ticklish.
- On-screen lines use `performer`, `tag`, `screen.now` facts. Keep them rare and specific.
- Chatter yields to orders: it must make sense on its own, at any quiet moment the conditions
  allow. Never write chatter that assumes what the next order is.
- Lies are allowed (foreshadowing with `ending.presentation=Lie`, false finishes). The lie must
  be plausible and the reveal line must exist (see `denied.after-lie`, `segment.after-false-finish`).

## Segments and aims

Read `docs/SEGMENTS.md` first. Most segments are improvised: she picks a purpose (ache, control,
overwhelm, starve, cool) and parameters from his state, and the steerer makes the steps. The
entries in `segments.json` with `"written": false` only name the shapes that come out, so lines can
refer to them; do not add step templates to those.

A **written segment** (`"written": true`) has a shape that is the point. Properties: `purpose`,
`tags`, `needs` (pacing, edges, methods, games), `fits` (the aims it may appear in; empty for any),
`share` of the points budget, `steps`, `persona` minimum dials, `weight` (how often its turn comes
up), and `step` (kind, speed, seconds, count, hold, method policy, and `reach: "hard"` to edge on
purpose on a grip that barely gets him there). A **written aim** (`"aims"`) is a `purpose`, a
`pattern` of purposes for its segments in order, `seconds` per segment, `chance`, and optionally
the `event` that brings it on.

Give a new written segment a `SegmentStart` module (`"when": ["#segment:<id>"]`) and, if its steps
need their own wrapper, an `Instruct` module with `"segment=<id>"`. Segment starts also carry
`#purpose:<id>`, `#role:advancing|supporting|side`, `#aim-start` (the first segment of a new aim)
and `#back` (returning to the aim after a break, a game or a side segment). Instructions carry
`#gap` (the hands-off after an edge), and `#again` or `#again-same` (the next edge of a hidden
count: the generated instruction is already "Again." or ends "and edge again", so do not prefix
another "Again"). An `EdgeRemark` with `#pace` carries `#quick`, `#quick-back` or `#slow` when he
reached the edge much faster or slower than she expected.

Torture overwhelms; it never hurts. Intensity and torture are different things: intensity is
pleasure that is hard to hold back from, torture is stimulation he wants to stop.

## Workflow

1. Read the neighbouring modules for the act so the new lines fit and don't duplicate.
2. Draft. Prefer fewer, better lines with conditions over many generic ones.
3. Validate and simulate:
   ```bash
   dotnet test tests/AIDome.Tests --filter "FullyQualifiedName~TranscriptTests"
   ```
   `TheShippedLinesAndSegmentsAreValid` checks ids, acts, conditions, slots, dashes and stories.
4. Read transcripts. Set `AIDOMME_TRANSCRIPTS` to a folder and rerun the same command; each
   simulated session (modes, users who beg, fail, slip, only edge) is written as Markdown with
   her plan and how it changed. Set `AIDOMME_SETTINGS_FROM` to the user's Cove connection string
   to simulate with their real settings and library (read-only). Read several end to end and look for repeats, lines that
   contradict what just happened, awkward joins between chatter and orders, and stories cut off.
5. Report which modules you added or changed, and quote two or three lines from a transcript.
